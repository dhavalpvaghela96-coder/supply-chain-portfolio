Executive Summary

This analysis examines demand and inventory patterns in Canadian fabricated metal product manufacturing (NAICS 332) from 2015 to 2025 using Statistics Canada Table 16-10-0047-01. Post-COVID demand recovery was faster and larger than the preceding decline — new orders surged 23% year-over-year in 2021 and a further 17% in 2022, while unfilled orders climbed to levels $1 billion above pre-COVID baselines and remained elevated through 2023. A production planner relying on pre-2020 demand baselines would have systematically under-ordered materials — resulting in exactly the kind of floor-level shortages and queue buildup that define the post-COVID supply chain crisis.

All 3 Metrics · 2015–2025 ($M)
2020 COVID Shock

The fabricated metal sector experienced a sharp, simultaneous decline across all three metrics in 2020. New orders fell the hardest at −46.8% — customers immediately halted procurement at the onset of lockdowns. Sales declined −39.8%, while unfilled orders dropped only −18.3%, showing that manufacturers maintained a large carry-forward backlog even as new demand evaporated.

Post-COVID Recovery · 2021–2023

The recovery was not gradual — it was a step change. New orders grew 23.2% in a single year (2021), followed by another 17.3% in 2022. By 2022, new orders reached $5.30B — 36% above the 2019 pre-COVID baseline of $3.91B. Any replenishment system operating on historical averages would lag behind by at least one full planning cycle, creating immediate material shortfalls.

The Backlog Never Cleared

Average unfilled orders in 2022–2023 settled at approximately $8.1B — $1 billion above the 2019 baseline of $7.1B. Despite record order volumes, manufacturers could not fully clear the demand pipeline. The unfilled-to-new-orders ratio remained above 1.8 through 2023 — meaning for every $1 of new orders received, manufacturers still carried $1.80 in unfulfilled backlog.

Root Cause
1

Replenishment parameters were set during or before 2020 — a period of suppressed demand. Safety stock calibrated to quiet conditions is structurally too low for the post-COVID environment.

2

The 2021 demand snapback was not gradual. A 23% YoY surge creates an immediate gap between what the system orders and what the floor needs.

3

Unfilled orders remain elevated industrywide — meaning supplier lead times are also under pressure, compounding the material shortage.

Recommendations
1

Recalibrate reorder points using 2021–2023 demand averages as the new baseline, not pre-COVID figures.

2

Increase safety stock for high-velocity SKUs to account for higher demand variability and extended supplier lead times.

3

Implement a quarterly demand review cycle to detect YoY growth trends before they cause floor-level shortages.

4

Build supplier lead time assumptions on current unfilled orders data rather than historical norms.

Limitations
No SKU-Level Visibility
The dataset does not identify which specific material categories experienced the greatest demand shifts. SKU-level analysis would require internal company data.
National Aggregate vs. Single Company
Industry-wide trends may not reflect any individual manufacturer. A company with a specialized customer base may have experienced different timing or magnitude of changes.
Conclusion

Post-COVID demand in Canadian fabricated metal manufacturing recovered faster, climbed higher, and stayed elevated longer than the preceding decline. A production planner operating on pre-2020 replenishment assumptions would be systematically underprepared — ordering too little, too late, against suppliers who are themselves backlogged. The current material shortages are the predictable outcome of applying quiet-year planning logic to a high-demand environment. Recalibrating reorder points, increasing safety stock, and establishing a regular demand review process are the concrete steps needed to realign production planning with post-COVID market reality.
